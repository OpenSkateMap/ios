# ComponentKit

**Purpose:** Small UIKit pieces shared by more than one screen, laid out with PinLayout, and how text is measured.
**Tier:** UI
**Project deps:** `SpotModelKit`, `ImageKit` (`ClipCache`) (packages: PinLayout, `SwiftMacros`; tests: `SpotModelKit`, `CustomDump`)
**Public Factory keys:** none
**Entry points:**
- `FormStackView` — views one under another at full width, each as tall as `sizeThatFits` says (or a fixed height); the short forms (the New Spot sheet, review edits, Edit Profile, reports) lay out with it.
- `UIButton.Configuration.primaryCapsule(title:)` — the one primary action on a screen: a label-colored capsule (white in dark mode, black in light). `secondaryCapsule(title:)` is its partner on a gray fill (Add Another, the credit editor). `UIButton.capsuleHeight(fitting:)` sizes either: 52 pt, taller when Dynamic Type wraps the title; lay capsules out with it, never a fixed 52.
- `UIColor.inverseLabel` — white in light mode, black in dark: content on a `.label` fill (the primary capsule, the spot sheet's Directions tile). Never `.systemBackground` there; it goes translucent on glass sheets.
- `UIScrollView.softEdges()` — soft scroll edge effects on every edge; chain it wherever a scroll view (or collection, table or text view) is made. iOS 27 defaults to the hard style under bars; lint `soft_scroll_edges` enforces it.
- `UIImage.resolved(with:)` — a dynamic image's variant for some traits as a plain image. A map snapshot carries both appearances, and an image view showing it, or a context drawing it, picks one again from whatever traits it has then: on a glass sheet that showed the light map in dark mode (Ertem, 2026-10-20). Every map snapshot goes through it with the traits it was asked for.
- `UIImage.tile(glyph:fill:side:glyphScale:tintsGlyph:)` — a settings-row icon drawn like an app icon: a 29 pt continuous-corner square (scaled with the text size, as iOS's own settings icons are, unless `side` is given) filled with one color plus a soft top-to-bottom sheen, or a diagonal gradient (`TileFill`), a hairline rim so dark tiles read on dark rows, and the glyph centered in white. `symbolTile(systemName:color:side:)` is the same with an SF Symbol. Every settings icon goes through it, so they share one size and corner.
- `UISheetPresentationController.setSheetLevel(_:)` / `hidesSheet(at:)` — sheet levels: a sheet names its level, and a sheet presented above it with `hidesSheet(at:)` hides it while it's up (it slides down as the new one rises, follows a swipe on the new one, and comes back when it closes), so one sheet shows at a time. Private API (`_setSheetID:`, `_setHiddenAncestorSheetID:`, in iOS 26.5's and 27's UIKitCore): names as `#Obfuscate` literals, the setter's type encoding checked first, and sheets stack the public way on a mismatch.
- `OptionSheetViewController` — a small option sheet: a bold title (title1) with a glass ✕ on its row, a short explanation, the content view, one white Confirm (dim until `isConfirmEnabled`; `setConfirmTitle(_:)` renames it, `setTitle(_:)` the title, for a step that changes it). Sized to its content, tinted by `TintedBackgroundView` (a sheet's background washed with a colour). Confirm runs `onConfirm` (spinning in its capsule meanwhile) and closes; a throw keeps it open with a failure toast, a `CancellationError` keeps it open silently. A sheet that goes step by step sets `closesOnConfirm = false` (its owner closes it) and a `secondaryAction` (Previous), a second capsule on Confirm's leading side. `onClose` runs once it has closed, by Confirm or not (New Spot's Cover Frame waits on it). New Spot's option sheets, Support and Discover's Filters use it. Its corners are the system's (no `preferredCornerRadius`): iOS 26 floats a sheet below full height inside the screen with corners concentric to it. While it floats, Confirm sits as far from the sheet's bottom edge as from its sides (20 pt), so the capsule nests in those corners (HIG: even margins, nested radius = outer minus margin); against the screen's edge it clears the home indicator, and the keyboard over it (Ertem, 2026-10-14: the bottom looked off). Content that doesn't fit scrolls under Confirm, which sits in a `UIScrollEdgeElementContainerInteraction` container so the scroll view's soft bottom edge effect blurs what passes behind it; scrolled to the end, the content keeps its 24 pt above Confirm (Ertem, 2026-10-20: a hard cut flush with the button read as a bug). With the keyboard up, once it and the sheet settle, the focused field scrolls to the top so what follows it shows above Confirm too (a credit's permission switch sat under Previous / Next).
- `LoopingVideoView` — a muted looping clip over its poster frame: the poster until the clip can draw (then the clip fades in, never a black frame), and instead of it while `isPlaying` is off. Makes its player on first play, pauses in the background, resumes in the foreground, never keeps the screen awake; hidden from VoiceOver. Sign-in's footage and the welcome screen's cards use it.
- `LoopingClipPlayer` and `ClipLayerView` — what `LoopingVideoView` is made of, for a clip whose poster lives elsewhere: the player (looping, muted unless `isMuted` is turned off, pauses in the background and resumes in the foreground, never keeps the screen awake; `seek(to:)`, `observePosition(every:_:)` while its token is kept, `hasSound()`) and a view that draws it, transparent until its first frame is ready. One player can feed several views at once, so a clip moves between screens without starting over; it goes when the last view lets go. `init(url:cache:)` with ImageKit's `ClipCache` plays the clip's file when the cache has it, and otherwise streams the URL while the cache downloads it for the next play; the asset is chosen once, as the player is made, so it never changes under a playing clip, and the cache keeps the file until the player goes. Without a cache it streams, as sign-in's footage and the review cards do.
- `ClipSound` — the app's one audio session owner. Until a clip's sound is on, the category is `.ambient`, set before the first clip plays: muted clips mix with other audio and never stop it. `takeAudio()` takes the audio as any video does (`.playback`, `.moviePlayback`: through the silent switch, pausing other audio); `handBackAudio()` gives it back (`notifyOthersOnDeactivation`, so other audio resumes). One worker applies the newest wish off the main thread, where `setActive` can block. Deactivating the session stops every player in it (`setActive`'s documented busy case), so muting while the audio is taken is the player's own `isMuted`, never a session change, and `handBackAudio()` waits until no clip is playing (each `LoopingClipPlayer` reports playing, pausing and going away), or the app leaves the screen, which pauses them; once it settles (`didSettle`) any player meant to be playing starts again.
- `ClipAutoplay` — the one rule for clips playing by themselves: Auto-Play Video Previews on and Low Power Mode off (`isAllowed`, `changeNotifications`). The spot's cover card and the review cards follow it.
- `ClipBadgeView` — a photo that has a clip: a small glass capsule with `play.fill` and its length in monospaced digits ("0:12", `lengthText(milliseconds:)`: the system's minutes and seconds, rounded to the nearest second); `isCompact` for a thumbnail. VoiceOver hears "Video, 12 seconds" (`accessibilityText(milliseconds:)`). New Spot's photo tiles and the review cards use it.
- `PhotoPicker` — where a photo comes from, as on the web: Take Photo (only where there's a camera), Photo Library (PHPicker, `limit` at once) and Choose File (Files, any image). Hands back each file's bytes as stored (`nil`, logged, when unreadable); the caller's preparation (ContributionsKit's `PhotoUploadManager.prepare`) turns any image ImageIO reads into the upright, metadata-free JPEG ≤ 2048 px the server takes. New Spot's Add Photos, Edit Profile's photo and collection photos use it; the owner keeps it alive. `init(presenter:limit:allowsVideos:onStart:)` hands each file over as it arrives instead of all at once: `onStart` hears what each place holds (`Kind`: photo or video) and the `Source` (camera, library, files) once the picker is off screen, so it can present (New Spot shows a preparing tile per pick and asks who took them, except for the camera's), and the receiver it returns gets each file in order as a `Pick`. With `allowsVideos` (New Spot and Suggest an Edit only) the library shows images and videos and Files takes movies; Take Photo stays photo-only, and a Live Photo stays a photo. A video arrives as `.video(URL)`: a copy in the temporary folder, made inside the provider's handler (its own file goes when the handler returns), never read into memory, and the receiver's to remove.
- `UIFont.preferredFont(forTextStyle:weight:compatibleWith:)` — a weighted system font that still scales with Dynamic Type, for the text size of the traits UIKit is resolving (`UITraitCollection.current` by default): a button configuration's title transformer runs with its button's, so its title scales with the button, not the app (a large-text snapshot showed big glyphs over small titles).
- `TextLayout` + `Label` — text measured without a view: TextKit 1, no line-fragment padding, no font leading, the line limit in the container (paragraphs wrap by word so the container truncates), rounded up to whole points. `Label` is the app's only label (lint `uilabel_instance`): a UILabel whose `sizeThatFits` is its `textLayout` (runs without a font, such as an attachment and the space beside it, measured in the label's `font`, as UILabel draws them: TextKit's Helvetica 12 left New Spot's nearby warning a line short), kept for the last width until the text, font, line limit or text size change. A one-line label reports its whole width whatever width it's asked to fit, as UILabel does: PinLayout's `sizeToFit()` measures at the current frame, so a capped width would never grow back (a score badge stuck on "…"). Layout code measures text through it, never UILabel's own sizing (Ertem, 2026-10-14). `linesAtAccessibilitySizes` is the line limit at accessibility text sizes (0: as many as the text needs), for a label that would otherwise cut what it says short there; other sizes keep the `numberOfLines` set, and `numberOfLines` reads back the limit in effect.
- `ToggleChipsView` — toggle chips (at least 32 pt tall, over HIG's 28 pt minimum) that wrap onto as many lines as they need (`refresh()` re-reads the selection): New Spot's obstacles, Discover's Filters sheet.
- `FlowLayout.frames(for:width:spacing:centered:)` — chips in lines, a new line when the next doesn't fit, each line as tall as its tallest: `ToggleChipsView`, the spot page's obstacles, a profile's handles (centred). `x` runs from the leading edge: pin with `start(frame.minX)`.
- `RowLayout.frames(for:width:spacing:stacks:displayScale:height:)` — pieces sharing a row (each an equal share, all as tall as the tallest) or, with `stacks` (accessibility text sizes), one under another at full width, each as tall as it measures; sizes round up to whole pixels as PinLayout's do, so an equal share lands where a chain of `after(of:)` pins put it: the spot sheet's action tiles, Appearance's tiles, a collection's Show on Map and Share, the review queue's Approve and Reject. `x` runs from the leading edge: pin with `start(frame.minX)`.
- `UIView.followLayoutDirection()` — the one launch call for right to left: PinLayout's start and end (and `layoutRow`) follow each view's own layout direction instead of PinLayout's default, left to right everywhere. `AppDelegate` calls it before the scene makes its window, and each snapshot suite in its `init()`.
- `UIView.physicalInsets(_:)` — `NSDirectionalEdgeInsets` as left and right in the direction PinLayout gives `start` here (`isLTR()`), for what only takes physical insets: the toast's padding (`wrapContent(padding:)`), ContributeKit's `InsetLabel` (Your Spots' badge, New Spot's photo credits and nearby warning) as it draws.
- `UIScrollView.layoutRow(_:spacing:insets:size:)` — a horizontally scrolling row from the leading edge: each view at the size `size` gives it (measure with `sizeThatFits`, never from last pass's frames), `spacing` apart inside directional `insets`, and `contentSize` to match. The views go in from the left; right to left the scroll view flips and each view flips back, so offsets, paging and snapping keep their left-to-right math (UICollectionView flips its layout's coordinates instead, with no transform). Asserts the launch call was made. Discover's filter chips and collection cards, the spot page's videos, and the photo pagers of the spot's cover and the review card.
- `UITraitCollection.trailingTextAlignment` — `.right` left to right, `.left` right to left: text set against the trailing edge, which `NSTextAlignment` has no case for. Set it in `updateProperties()`, which follows trait changes: New Spot's row values, the bio counter, the sheet editor's count. A literal `.left` or `.right` is lint `pinlayout_left_right`.
- `UITextField.runsLeftToRight()` — a field whose text reads left to right in any language (forced left to right, aligned left: `.natural` would still align an empty field's placeholder by the app's language): Edit Profile's username and handles (`FieldRowView`'s `handle:`), Add Video's link, Add People's @username or email.
- `PullToRefreshControl` — a `UIRefreshControl` whose spinner stays at least 0.5 s and ends only once the finger lifts (ending it mid-pull drops its inset and the pull snaps back); `refresh` returns what to show after the minimum, such as a failure toast. The screen's `scrollViewDidEndDragging` calls `fingerLifted()`, as the screens did before: ended from the pan gesture's own target instead, the scroll view kept the refresh inset after the spinner ended (checked on the simulator). Profile and Contribute use it.
- `UIBarButtonItem.spinner()` / `UICellAccessory.spinner()` — a spinning bar item or trailing cell accessory while something runs; `UINavigationItem.showRightItem(_:)` swaps a bar's trailing item (Send ↔ spinner) only when it changes.
- `BadgeLabel` — a capsule label with room around its text: a profile's role.
- `TextViewListCell` — a text field in an inset-grouped list: one line (Return ends it) or a paragraph that grows as it's typed (`minimumHeight` for room to write), a placeholder and a character limit. Edit Collection's name and description, Report's note.
- `UITabBarController.updateTabBarPlacement()` — iPad's tab bar at the top while an iPhone lies on its side (Ertem, 2026-10-19): compact height, the device's top facing left or right, and UIKit keeping the bar at the bottom (a foldable moves it to a side itself; that check is unverified until a Duo runtime exists). In portrait it doesn't fit (it pages the tabs), so only there. Private API (iOS 26 and 27): the adaptive visual style's `configuration.supportsFloatingTabBar`, then `_updateBarAvailabilityAnimated:`, since its own refresh runs only when the width class changes (a Pro Max is regular width on its side already); the width is forced regular through `traitOverrides` (the trait combination Pro Max screens already lay out for). Names as `#Obfuscate` literals, each accessor's type encoding checked; on a mismatch the bar stays at the bottom. The app calls it from `viewIsAppearing` and on size-class changes, and `tabBarPlacementDidSettle()` alongside a size transition, registered before `super`'s so it runs ahead of the children's layout: a bottom bar coming back from the top mid-rotation keeps its landscape height (65 pt instead of 83) in the children's safe area until it's shown again, and correcting it only at the end made Discover's panel land low and jump (Ertem, 2026-10-20). Turning the flag off never forces the refresh; the width change does it. `isTabBarAtTop` says whether it did.
- `NavigationController` — the tabs' stacks: the status bar follows the top screen, and under a tab bar at the top every screen's title sits beside the back button in the tab bar's row (`UINavigationItem.style` `.browser`), as iPad lays it out; centred, UIKit gives it a row of its own below the bar, which also changed the bar's height as the title came and went (Ertem, 2026-10-20). Portrait keeps `.navigator`.
- `UIWindow.deviceTopEdge` — the window edge the device's top (and its sensor housing) faces, from the window's geometry, which a rotation's layout and trait changes already have; the scene's interface orientation updates later.
- `UISheetPresentationController.placeBesideMap()` — a sheet over the map keeps to the leading edge in a wide window (iOS 27 `preferredPlacement`, edge-attached in compact height, sized as a form sheet), full width when narrow. The spot sheet, the sheets it opens and Discover's sheets use it.
- `ActionPopoverController` — a few actions (title, optional detail line, symbol) in a popover anchored to the row that was tapped (as wide as its titles need when they're all it has, else 320 pt), a popover on iPhone too (Ertem, 2026-10-19: a popover, not a menu), its rows clear over the popover's glass; it closes before the chosen action runs. Add People's roles and Profile's Copy Email / Copy User ID use it.
- `ToastView` — a glass capsule that drops in from the top: an icon (`ToastViewPredefinedIcon` success / failure) or a spinner, and a centred title with an optional line under it, over everything in its own window (`ToastWindow`, above alerts, takes no touch but a toast's button). Events (`ToastViewEvent`: hidden, icon, loading) each move it on from the last one, and the same event again is dropped; a burst within an animation's length lands as its last event. Make one per notice, `update(with:)` it and send `.hidden`, or `await show(_:for:)`; a failure is `ToastView.showFailure(_:subtitle:)`. `show(_:actionTitle:for:action:)` adds one button after the text (Undo after a takedown): the only touch the toast window takes; tapping it hides the toast, then runs the action. Toasts stack (Ertem, 2026-10-19: many errors at once): the newest in front, up to two more peeking below it, each 14 pt lower and 5% smaller at the front one's size with its text hidden; each leaves on its own timer and the rest move up. A toast saying exactly what one on screen says isn't shown again, so a burst of the same failure is one toast. The window is visible only while it holds a toast. The title is announced to VoiceOver. With Reduce Motion a toast fades in and out where it shows, and the stack's scale doesn't animate.
- `CollectionArtworkView` — a collection's artwork as a music app shows an album's: its photo filling the view (its own, else its newest spot's: `CollectionLook.pictureKey(cover:)`), or without one its colour (`CollectionColor.tones`, the server's pick) with its kind's symbol (`CollectionKind.pictureSymbol`) in white. Discover's collection cards and the collection page's hero use it.
- `SpotKind` / `SpotCondition` appearance — title, SF Symbol and colour, shared by the map pins and the spot sheet. `SpotObstacle.title(of:)` / `name(of:)` — an obstacle in the app's language: the 12 common ones (`SpotObstacle.Common`) have a title for chips, menus and filters ("Manual Pads") and a name for inside a list ("manual pads"; German capitalises it, "Treppen"), in the words local skaters use; a spelling that differs only by case or a plural "s" ("gap", `SpotObstacle.Common(stored:)`) gets the common one's words where the language has its own, and stays as stored in English; an obstacle someone typed stays as typed, capitalized on a chip as English always showed it. What's stored, synced and filtered stays the canonical lowercase English (Ertem, 2026-10-21). `VideoProvider` appearance — the platform's name and its glyph before a thumbnail.

**Ownership map:** none; components hold no app state.

## Rules

- Add a component here only when a second screen needs it; until then it stays in its feature.
- System colors only (`.label`, `.systemOrange`, …). Resolve dynamic colors per trait before handing them to a layer.
- Decorative views hide themselves from VoiceOver.
